# Robot Configurations

Robots come in various configurations depending on their intended tasks and environments. Understanding these configurations is essential for selecting the right robot for a specific application.

## Serial Manipulators

Serial manipulators are the most common type of robot arm, consisting of joints connected in series. They're widely used in manufacturing for tasks like welding, painting, and assembly.

### Common Serial Configurations

- **Cartesian**: Uses prismatic joints for linear motion along x, y, and z axes
- **Cylindrical**: Combines prismatic and revolute joints to operate in cylindrical space
- **Spherical**: Two intersecting revolute joints with a prismatic joint
- **Articulated**: Multiple revolute joints with non-intersecting axes
- **SCARA**: Selective Compliance Assembly Robot Arm - ideal for horizontal plane tasks

## Parallel Manipulators

Parallel manipulators have multiple kinematic chains connecting the base to the end-effector. They offer high rigidity and precision but limited workspace.

### Examples

- **Stewart Platform**: Six linear actuators arranged in parallel, used for flight simulators
- **Delta Robot**: Three arms connected to a common base, used for high-speed pick-and-place

## Mobile Robots

Mobile robots can move within their environment. They're classified based on their locomotion method:

### Wheeled Robots

- **Differential Drive**: Two independently controlled wheels with castor for balance
- **Ackermann Steering**: Like cars, with steerable front wheels
- **Mecanum Wheels**: Omnidirectional motion without changing robot heading

### Legged Robots

- **Bipedal**: Two legs, mimicking human locomotion
- **Quadrupedal**: Four legs, offering stability and versatility
- **Hexapodal**: Six legs, providing excellent stability

### Tracked Robots

Using continuous tracks like tanks, these robots excel in rough terrain and offer good traction.

## Hybrid Configurations

### Mobile Manipulators

Combining mobility with manipulation, these robots can navigate to different locations while performing manipulation tasks.

### Climbing Robots

Designed to navigate vertical or inverted surfaces, they're used for inspection of structures like bridges and buildings.

## Configuration Selection Factors

When selecting a robot configuration, consider:

- **Workspace requirements**: Size, shape, and reach needed
- **Payload capacity**: Weight of objects to be manipulated
- **Precision requirements**: Accuracy needed for the application
- **Environment**: Indoor, outdoor, hazardous, etc.
- **Cost constraints**: Budget limitations for the project
- **Maintenance requirements**: Ease of repair and service
